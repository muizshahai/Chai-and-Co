A chai house that stays up with you.

How Chai & Co started and how every cup gets made, from the first pot in the morning to the last one poured after dark.

Why we opened

Growing up in a small town in Texas, the first thing I noticed in our house each morning was usually the smell of chai on the stove, because in my household it was never treated as a special drink so much as the way a day was supposed to start. I still have a cup every morning, and even now the few minutes I spend waiting for it to come together feel like the part of the day that belongs to me before everything else begins.

When I started spending more time in Dallas, I found myself wanting a place that made chai the way I was used to drinking it at home, brewed slowly on the stove rather than poured from a carton of concentrate, where I could sit with friends late into the evening without the night having to revolve around a bar.

Chai & Co is my attempt at building that place, a chai house that brews every pot from scratch and keeps its doors open late enough that a conversation over a second cup never has to end early. My hope is that anyone who walks in feels the same comfort I felt in our kitchen growing up, where a warm cup was always less about the tea itself and more about the people sitting around it.

Muiz Shah, founder

How every cup is made

Every pot is brewed on the stove instead of from a concentrate, and since each one takes about twelve minutes, we keep a fresh pot going through the night.

  1. Crack the spices

    Green cardamom, fresh ginger and a little clove go into the mortar right before brewing so the oils are still sharp.

  2. Simmer the tea

    Black tea leaves boil with water and spice until the color turns the deep red of a brick. This is where the strength comes from.

  3. Bring in the milk

    Milk goes in and the pot rises twice. We let it fall back between each rise so the chai thickens without scorching.

  4. Pull and pour

    We pour from height between two pots to cool it slightly and build a light froth, then strain it straight into your cup.

Where it comes from

Tea

Strong Kenyan CTC black tea, the same kind most Pakistani households brew, picked for body so it holds up to milk and spice.

Spice

Cardamom from Guatemala, one of the largest growers in the world, plus Kashmiri saffron for the zafran.

Milk

Whole milk from a dairy outside Sulphur Springs, Texas. Oat and almond milk are always on hand.

After dark at Chai & Co

The room changes after 9pm, when the lamps go down and the long tables fill up with people who came in for one cup and ended up staying for a few more.

  • Thursday open mic. Poetry, guitar, stand-up. Sign up at the counter by 8pm.
  • Study Sundays. Quiet tables from 6pm to close, outlets everywhere, half-price second cup with a student ID.
  • Chai catering. We bring a brass pot, cups and a server to weddings, iftars, office events and late study sessions across DFW.

Questions we get a lot

How much caffeine is in a cup?

A regular karak or masala chai has roughly half the caffeine of a drip coffee. The dirty chai adds an espresso shot, so it lands closer to a latte.

Can you make it dairy free?

Yes. Every chai can be made with oat or almond milk. Kashmiri pink tastes best with oat because it keeps the pink color.

Why is the Kashmiri chai pink?

It is brewed from green tea with a pinch of baking soda and salt, then aerated for a long time. The reaction with the milk turns it a soft rose color.

Do you take reservations?

Not for tables, but groups of eight or more can message us a day ahead and we will set aside a long table.
